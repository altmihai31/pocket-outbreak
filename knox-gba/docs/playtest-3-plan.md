## Decisions (mine, not asked)

- **Lit sprites, in the code:** a sprite's centre is tested against each light that is on: the lit room's rectangle, the holder's glow (a radius), the beams (a distance and an angle from the apex: the same shape as the beam sprite), fire glows (a radius). Few lights and at most a few dozen sprites, so a handful of comparisons each; checked against the frame budget on bench 10. A sprite half in a light switches whole (a 16 px sprite: barely seen). The windows keep lighting the ground.
- **The MENU page:** opens on its two entries; A opens one, B goes back to them. SETTINGS is today's page unchanged. On the title the same page, reached from the MENU line (and the Select line without a save).
- **Guide topics** (about a dozen, short, written from the game's real numbers): controls; staying alive (hunger, thirst, sleep, the moodles); zombies (sight, noise, crouching); fighting (swings, stamina, pushing); guns (aiming, reloading, jams, noise); wounds and infection; looting and carrying; building and barricades; food, cooking, traps and farming; animals; night, light and the power going off; vehicles; saving. Up/Down choose the topic, Left/Right turn its pages. Kept in ROM; no save change.
- **Gun frames:** the new body frames go in the player sheet (3 facings x aim, and low-ready stand, steps and crouch). Sprite tiles are streamed per frame, so the sheet's size costs ROM only. The gun pictures get grips placed where the new hands are; diagonal aims use the nearest facing's body with the gun at its exact angle.
- **No save format change** for any of it.

## Stages

1. **Lit sprites:** the light test in code for every world sprite (player, held items, zombies, corpses, animals, vehicles, effects); bench 10 checked (lit room, flashlight, campfire, headlights) with the window's sprite effect switched off on purpose, to stand in for the device.
2. **MENU and GUIDE:** mockup; the tab and the title entry renamed, the MENU page; the guide's pages and text.
3. **Guns in hand:** mockup (every gun, 4 facings, rest and aim); the body frames; the poses and grips; checked on bench 11 (every gun fired).
4. **Wolves:** a trace bench (bench 15's wolves: position, speed, picture and facing every frame while circling and darting); the fixes; the trace again.

## Progress

**Stage 1 built 2026-11-24** (untested on device):

- **The test** (`kx_light.h/.cpp`): `light::clear_lit` / `add_lit_rect` / `add_lit_disc` / `add_lit_beam` hold what the lights cover this frame, in world pixels; `light::lit(point)` tests a sprite's centre (a bounding box of all the lights first, then the room, the glows, the beams); `light::shade` now leaves a sprite out of the fade when `lit` says so (sprites without a camera, rain and the smoke marker, are only darkened). The shapes are the light frames' own (`fx.py`): glows 28 and 14 px, beams 23 degrees each side and cut by their circle, as drawn (the flashlight at its swept angle, the far beam of Eagle-eyed).
- **Filled:** `game::_update_lights` clears it each frame and adds the lit room (the same rectangle the renderer gets); `light_sprites::update` adds the beams and glows it draws. The sprites are shaded at the top of the next frame, from the positions the lights were worked out from.
- **Kept shading:** zombies on screen are shaded every night frame (all of them when the night begins or ends); corpses, animals and particles every frame while a light is on or has just gone off.
- **Checked** on bench 10 (`docs/playtest-3-light.png`): left, a build with every light window switched off, standing in for the device: the ground stays dark and the player, the car in the headlights and the player by the campfire are lit by the code alone; right, the normal build, as before. Cost against the same build with the test stubbed out: +0.4% CPU on average over the run, +1% at worst, peak unchanged (61%, 32 zombies).
- **A testing trap found:** restoring a source file with PowerShell's `Copy-Item` keeps the copy's older timestamp, so make kept the object built from the temporary version. Touch restored files before building.

**Stage 4 built 2026-11-25** (untested on device):

- **The trace:** bench 15 logs every wolf every frame from 1500 to 2000 (`KXWTRACE`: state, position, speed, real movement, picture, side, detour), and its survivor now stands, then walks east (1750) and south (1870) while the pack circles. `tools/wolf_trace.py <log>` counts, per wolf per second of circling or darting: facing flips, view changes (side, front, back), stop-go, speed jumps, sharp turns and detours.
- **Before** (per second): facing flips 6.3, view changes 4.0, stop-go 11.6, speed jumps 14.1. As read from the code, plus one more: a wolf pushing into an obstacle slid along it at a sixth of its speed with its legs running at full speed.
- **The fixes** (`kx_animals`): a circling wolf runs at a point gliding round the ring (`ring_heading`, a whole degree every 2.8 frames instead of a 1.4° jump every 4) at a speed of the gap over 8 frames (capped at the pacing speed, the running speed when far), its velocity easing a quarter of the way to that each frame (`wolf_pursuit_frames`, `wolf_turn_frames`): it settles a few px behind the point at the ring's pace and never stops. Every animal's picture follows how it really moved (`animal::moved`, smoothed over two frames) rather than where it pushed. A wolf turns its picture round only when moving across faster than 0.3 px a frame (`wolf_face_milli`). The side/front/back views have a margin (side to front or back past about 63 degrees, back below about 48), and an animal creeping under 0.08 px a frame keeps its view (`view_still_milli`). A blocked wolf turns to the side it circles every time, not alternating sides.
- **After:** facing flips 0.3, view changes 0.9 (the turns of its path and its darts), stop-go 0.1, speed jumps 0.2, sharp turns 0.2. The bench's wolf phases play out as before: noticed at 78 px, two darts and bites, the pack runs after a wolf is shot and from the campfire, the axe kill and butchering (8 raw meat, 2 hides); no missed frames. Bench 16 (the census) runs as before.
